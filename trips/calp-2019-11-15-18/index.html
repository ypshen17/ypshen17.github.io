Methods

Oysterbnb MaxEnt adaptation

Rebuilt the Oysterbnb species-distribution stack using CMEMS salinity, Copernicus turbidity, slope rasters, and 2018 Posidonia presence points to output daily suitability hexes.

Salinity + DO transects

Logged YSI ProDSS vertical profiles every 0.5 m at three seawall stations, filtered against CMEMS reanalysis to capture dawn gradients.

Photogrammetry quadrats

Captured 1 m2 photo quadrats with dual strobes, processed in Agisoft, and counted shoots/epiphytes via ImageJ to validate habitat quality.

Acoustic comfort rubric

Reused Oysterbnb comfort scoring (sound, wind, crowd, shade, seating) to evaluate evening boardwalk usability for residents vs. visitors.

Reflections

  • Oysterbnb's MaxEnt stack translated well; salinity proved the main predictor with bathymetry acting as a secondary constraint.
  • Evening crowd counts plateaued despite weekend influx - boardwalk retains local rhythms when acoustic programming remains acoustic-scale.

Field Note

By dawn I could hear Calpe Rock bouncing a low 92 Hz hum back at the seawall. The salinity profile stayed just shy of 38 PSU—stable enough for the MaxEnt suitability hex to glow orange in the storymap. Every transect inched Oysterbnb’s New York workflow closer to the Mediterranean.

Observation Highlights

  • Salinity and DO matched Copernicus rasters within ±0.3 PSU / 0.4 mg/L, validating the suitability transfer.
  • Posidonia shoot density averaged 565 shoots/m² across three quadrats, with epiphyte load controlled under 15%.
  • Evening comfort score tracked Oysterbnb’s rubric: crowd counts rose, but acoustic levels stayed below 65 dB(A) thanks to softer busker programming.

Next Iteration

Return in peak summer to log stormwater pulses, test jellyfish season comfort adjustments, and invite the local sailing school to co-produce a visitor code of conduct grounded in the habitat suitability data.

Limits

  • Posidonia counts taken in fair weather; storm-induced turbidity remains unmapped.
  • Comfort rubric excludes seasonal jellyfish blooms that may shift harbor use in summer months.
