The city is a dynamic, unfolding score.  
It is an ever-evolving composition of time, space, and perception. Each street, every sound, every whisper contributes to a broader, collective rhythm that transcends any architect's design. 

The concept of "Universal Composition" is proposed by the Universal Composition Laboratory at UCL. It challenges traditional, visual-centred urban design by recognising the city as a multisensory phenomenon, where every element, such as sound, breath, and matter, exists in resonance with the others.

The city's true complexity cannot be captured by geometry alone. Universal Composition proposes a broader definition of form that integrates sound, human movement, and the forces of nature as essential co-creators. By embracing this framework, we open the city to multiplicity, interdisciplinary collaboration, and an improvisational process, rejecting rigid control in favour of dynamic, ever-shifting relationships.

If we go back to architecture, the concept finds resonance in Kazunari Sakamoto's architectural philosophy of kumigumi (composition/assemblage). In his *Studies of Architectural Composition*, Sakamoto critiques the dominance of rigid, "strong-form" approaches to space, and instead advocates for a "weak-form" architecture, which is adaptive, open, and deeply attuned to the poetics of the everyday.

This framework doesn't reduce design to geometry or form. Instead, it recognizes architecture as a soft, permeable structure of relationships, where sound, breath, materiality, and memory are co-composers. Sakamoto's method bridges poetics of the ordinary with rigorous composition thinking, offering an alternative to the control-oriented, object-centric paradigms of contemporary design.

In this sense, **Universal Composition** is not a fixed system but an ongoing act of listening and assembling, act of composing space as one might compose music or poetry.  
Rhythmically, intuitively, and responsively.  
The city is not a static place but a living composition that breathes, listens, and responds. 

Like the image of soundwaves intersecting a skyline, the city's essence is not confined to the visual. It pulses with energy, shaping and being shaped by the sounds of life within it.

To me, it explains everything in life:
A drop falls in the fountain, creating ripples that reflect the sky.
A person who overturn the waterfall upside down, and shoot a fountain to the sky.
A train departs, its sound cascading through the station, merging with the hum of the city.
The people who have spent their lives with trains.
A child whispers to a wall, and the city listens, reverberating with echoes of the past and present.

The urban composition I envision invites participation rather than control.  
It is improvisational, intersubjective, and polyphonic
composed by time, by weather, by designers, by passersby, 

We are participants in a symphony of existence.
Together, we create the unrepeatable fountain of life.
