The Architecture of Absurdity

A vision of cracked laughter, fractured logic, and the spaces in between

We did not ask to laugh.

But we learned to, anyway,
in the shadows of diagrams, under the scaffolding of logic, between the lines of zoning codes and sound ordinances.

This is a vision of absurdity as urban condition:
not satire, but sincerity stretched thin.
A staircase to nowhere.
A monument to emptiness.
A song that sounds like a eulogy, yet dares to dance.

“내 삶은 엉터리 우울 뒤에서
내뱉어진 B급 우스개”
(My life — a B-grade joke uttered behind counterfeit sorrow)
— Lee Seung Yoon

In the absurd city, we walk crooked on purpose. We build a shadow museum. We sing funeral hymns at weddings, and throw confetti at farewells. These are not mistakes.
These are acts of refusal, nothing serious, but of performative normalcy.

The architecture of absurdity does not solve, it reveals: that sincerity often wears a clown’s mask, that the prettiest façade may hide collapse, that the city, in its overdesign, often forgets how to feel.

And yet, we persist. Not in hope, but in composition.
A cracked rhythm. A minor chord. A whisper of resistance.

“삶은 정말 코미디, 근데 가끔 하모니”
(Life is really a comedy, but sometimes, a harmony)

We design, knowing the drawing may be discarded.
We speak, knowing no plan may listen.
We laugh, not because it’s funny but because it matters.

Let comedy come. Let it fold us. Let it bite. Let it weep.
Let us build, in the absurd, a space that still dares to hold love.
