Universal Composition

The City as Unfolding Score

The city is a dynamic, unfolding score. It is an ever-evolving composition of time, space, and perception. Each street, every sound, every whisper contributes to a broader, collective rhythm that transcends any architect’s design. The concept of Universal Composition, proposed by the Universal Composition Laboratory at UCL, challenges traditional, visual-centred urban design by recognising the city as a multisensory phenomenon, where every element, such as sound, breath, and matter, exists in resonance with the others.

The city’s true complexity cannot be captured by geometry alone. Universal Composition proposes a broader definition of form that integrates sound, human movement, and the forces of nature as essential co-creators. By embracing this framework, we open the city to multiplicity, interdisciplinary collaboration, and an improvisational process, rejecting rigid control in favour of dynamic, ever-shifting relationships.

The city is not a static place; it is a living composition that breathes, listens, and responds. Like the image of soundwaves intersecting a skyline, the city’s essence is not confined to the visual. It pulses with energy, shaping and being shaped by the sounds of life within it.

To me, it explains everything in life: A drop falls in the fountain, creating ripples that reflect the sky. A train departs, its sound cascading through the station, merging with the hum of the city. A child whispers to a wall, and the city listens, reverberating with echoes of the past and present. We are not masters of space but participants in a symphony of existence. Together, we create the unrepeatable cadence of life.
