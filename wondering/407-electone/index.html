Unlike the piano or keyboard, the Electone is an electronic organ designed to simulate the dynamics of an entire orchestra.

It is a console of multiple manuals and foot pedals, where both hands and feet collaborate to layer melody, harmony, and rhythm simultaneously.

It challenges the linearity of performance:

  • The upper manual plays melodies or leads,
  • The lower manual carries harmony or counterpoint,
  • The pedals add bass or rhythm, anchoring the whole composition.

This multi-dimensionality makes the Electone less about reproducing piano music and more about orchestral imagination in real time.

It is an instrument of arrangement, layering, and improvisation, where the performer is also the conductor of textures.

For me, the Electone resonates with my fascination for composition as method:
like the city, it demands coordination between different registers, scales, and rhythms.

It is not about mastering one line of sound but orchestrating multiplicity into coherence.
